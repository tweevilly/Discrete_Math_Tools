How to type an expression

Both styles work, and you can mix them.

  • NOT: not P, !P, ~P, ¬P, or -P
  • AND: P and Q, P ^ Q, P * Q, P ∧ Q, or letters side by side like xy
  • OR: P or Q, P v Q, P + Q, or P ∨ Q
  • XOR: P xor Q or P ⊕ Q
  • Implies: P -> Q or P → Q
  • If and only if: P <-> Q or P ↔ Q
  • Constants: 1, 0, true, false

Order of operations, tightest first: NOT, AND, then OR and XOR, then implies, then if and only if. Use parentheses whenever you want to be sure.

Variables are single letters, and an expression can use up to 6 of them (64 rows).

Reading the result

A tautology is true in every row, like P or not P. A contradiction is false in every row, like P and not P. Anything else is contingent, meaning the answer depends on the inputs. Two expressions are equivalent when they give the same answer in every row, and the comparison above shows any rows where they differ.
